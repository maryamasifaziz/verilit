# How well do multilingual pretrained language models perform zero-shot cross-lingual transfer?

## Literature review

Multilingual pretrained language models demonstrate varying degrees of capability in zero-shot cross-lingual transfer, supported by specific architectural designs and training techniques. For neural machine translation, SixT utilizes a multilingual pretrained encoder featuring a two-stage training schedule, a position disentangled encoder, and a capacity-enhanced decoder [1]. Furthermore, model adaptation through continued pretraining enhances XLM-R's zero-shot cross-lingual performance on 10 Indigenous languages of the Americas, increasing average accuracy from 38.48% to 43.85% [2].

Across downstream natural language processing tasks, performance levels differ based on the model family and adaptation strategy. The XLM-RoBERTa model family consistently demonstrates superior performance in transferring discourse-level knowledge across languages with relatively minor degradation in zero-shot settings [3]. Additionally, prompt-tuning yields substantially better cross-lingual transfer than fine-tuning across natural language understanding datasets while utilizing only 0.1% to 0.3% tuned parameters [4]. Assessment of these capabilities relies on specific evaluation suites; for instance, ECLeKTic serves as a multilingual closed-book QA dataset to assess cross-lingual knowledge transfer using Wikipedia articles across 12 languages [5], and BUFFET unifies 15 diverse tasks across 54 languages in a sequence-to-sequence format for evaluating cross-lingual transfer [6].

Despite these advancements, low-resource languages and non-English contexts encounter notable limitations and failure modes. Cross-lingual and unsupervised methods struggle to preserve sentiment-specific information under zero-shot conditions for the low-resource language Oromo [7]. Moreover, evaluations of large language models such as CodeLLaMa and CodeGemma show significant disparities in code quality when processing non-English prompts during zero-shot cross-lingual transfer [8].
