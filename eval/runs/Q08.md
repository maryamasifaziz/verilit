# How do vision transformers compare with convolutional neural networks for image classification?

## Literature review

Vision Transformers (ViTs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) exhibit fundamental architectural differences and methodologies for image classification. CNNs suffer from spatial bias and small receptive fields [2]. In contrast, Vision Transformers feature more uniform representations across all layers through self-attention that enables early global information aggregation alongside residual connections propagating features [1], thereby exploiting global self-attention mechanisms for better contextual modeling and long-range spatial dependencies [2].

When comparing classification accuracy and reported results, studies highlight diverse outcomes across specific medical and general tasks. ResNet-50 achieved 98.37% accuracy on chest X-ray classification, DeiT-Small achieved 92.16% accuracy on brain tumor detection, and EfficientNet-B0 achieved 81.84% accuracy on skin cancer classification [3]. Additionally, Compact Convolutional Transformers achieved a classification accuracy of 92.49% and a micro-average ROC AUC of 0.9935 on a benchmark dataset of peripheral blood cell images [4]. 

Research benchmarking Vision Transformers against Convolutional Neural Networks relies on standard datasets such as ImageNet, COCO, and Cityscapes [5], while comparative evaluations of CNNs, Transformers, and hybrid models for image segmentation use benchmark datasets including Cityscapes, ADE20K, and BRATS [6]. Despite their strengths, Vision Transformers have significant limitations, including high computational complexity, memory demands, and scaling and data challenges that pose difficulties for deployment on resource-constrained devices [8]. Specifically, vision transformers demand larger training datasets and higher computational resources than convolutional neural networks because of the quadratic complexity inherent in their self-attention mechanism [7].
