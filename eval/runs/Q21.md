## Literature review

Machine learning and deep learning methods are widely utilized to detect phishing websites and emails. Various algorithms have been evaluated, including Decision Tree, Support Vector Machine, Naïve Bayes, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Extra Trees, K-Nearest Neighbors, and Multilayer Perceptron [1, 2]. Among these, comparative studies have identified both Random Forest and Extra Trees as achieving superior performance across metrics such as accuracy, precision, and F1-Score [1, 2].

Feature engineering and dataset selection play a foundational role in these detection systems. Studies have utilized URL-based feature extraction [4], incorporating 32 distinct features such as URL length, domain tokens, and the number of special characters [3]. These models rely on various datasets, including merged publicly available sources as well as the Mendeley and PhiUSIIL datasets [3, 4]. To measure effectiveness, evaluations employ metrics such as predictive accuracy, probability of detection, probability of miss-detection, probability of false alarm, explainability metrics, and F1-Score [2, 5, 6].

Despite high reported performance, machine learning-based phishing detection models encounter several current limitations and vulnerabilities. Key challenges include computational limitations, dataset biases, real-time adaptability issues, and adversarial attacks [7]. Specifically, models such as Phishpedia, VisualPhishNet, and the Stack model suffer a significant drop in detection rates when tested against adversarial phishing webpages generated by PhishOracle [8].
