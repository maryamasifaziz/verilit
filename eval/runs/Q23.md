# How do mixture-of-experts architectures scale language models efficiently?

## Literature review

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures efficiently scale Large Language Models by decoupling total parameters from computational cost [3]. Through this design, autoregressive MoE language models can achieve performance comparable to dense models while using approximately four times less compute under modest training budgets [4]. 

Regarding architectural designs and routing mechanisms, studies have introduced routing signatures—vector representations summarizing expert activation patterns across layers for specific prompts—tested using OLMoE-1B-7B-0125-Instruct [1]. Furthermore, analysis of MoE models reveals that multilingual processing is structured so that routing aligns with linguistic families, with expert utilization following a distinct layerwise pattern [2]. To assess these models, evaluations commonly utilize language modelling benchmarks such as the WikiText2, PTB, and C4 datasets with models like Mixtral 8x7B [5], as well as image classification datasets like MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100 for SNNL-augmented MoE approaches [6].

Despite their computational efficiency, the scalability of MoE models is significantly hindered by practical limitations. Expert parallelism heavily relies on all-to-all communication across devices, which typically constitutes a major portion of the total runtime [8]. MoE models are further constrained by high communication overhead stemming from all-to-all operations, low GPU utilization due to synchronous communication constraints, and complications that arise in heterogeneous GPU environments [7].
