## Literature review

To improve neural machine translation (NMT) for low-resource languages, various architectural modifications and modeling techniques are employed. Specifically, NMT can be adapted by introducing more local dependencies and utilizing word alignments to learn sentence reordering, which yields acceptable translations using a training size of only 70,000 tokens [1]. 

Data augmentation and transfer learning strategies further enhance performance in low-resource settings. Transfer learning approaches—such as training a high-resource parent model and transferring its parameters to a low-resource child model—improve baseline NMT models by an average of 5.6 BLEU points across four low-resource language pairs [2]. Furthermore, a hybrid combination of transfer learning and data augmentation featuring a self-supervised objective achieves the highest translation quality in extremely low-resource scenarios [3]. Joint methods that incorporate back-translation into parent-child and hierarchical transfer learning architectures also outperform single-method baselines on Uygur-Chinese and Turkish-English translation tasks [4]. 

Evaluation of these models utilizes diverse benchmark datasets and metrics. For instance, Bible translation tasks trained with NLLB achieve 35.1 and 31.6 BLEU scores for Austronesian and Trans-New Guinea language families, respectively [5], while evaluation gains for English-Tigrinya translation are validated using BLEU, chrF, and qualitative human evaluation [6]. 

Despite these advancements, deploying translation models for extremely low-resource languages encounters primary limitations and challenges. Languages such as Tigrinya remain underserved due to limited corpora, inadequate tokenization strategies, and the absence of standardized evaluation benchmarks [8]. Similarly, neural machine translation for low-resource local languages in Indonesia is heavily hindered by limited data availability and the pressing need for representative benchmarks [7].
