# How do sentiment analysis models handle sarcasm and irony?

*VeriLit (with verification) · gemini gemini-3.5-flash-lite · 8 claims, 8 verified, 0 unverified · 28 s*

## Literature review

Sentiment analysis models utilize various advanced techniques and architectures to handle sarcasm and irony. A modular deep learning framework combines Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs) and BERT to analyze linguistic, emotional, and contextual cues [1]. Additionally, an amalgamation of four parallel deep long-short term networks (pLSTM) achieves a training accuracy of 98.95% for sarcasm detection [2]. To evaluate sentiment analysis on such content, researchers utilize benchmark datasets and corpora including the TweetEval Irony and Sarcasm Corpus V2 [3], as well as the HurricaneSARC dataset, which consists of 15,000 tweets annotated for intended sarcasm in a disaster context [4].

Regarding performance and challenges, the figurative and creative nature of sarcasm poses a great challenge for affective computing systems performing sentiment analysis [5]. Furthermore, research indicates that large language models may fail to recognize sarcasm or irony in text, which highlights the complexity of sentiment analysis [6]. 

Despite these architectures, current sentiment models face notable limitations when processing figurative language and context. Traditional NLP techniques, such as lexicon-based methods and classical machine learning classifiers, struggle to interpret contextual nuances, domain-specific terminology, and subtle linguistic features like sarcasm [7]. Moreover, evaluations of Large Visual Language Models in Multimodal Sarcasm Analysis reveal key limitations, including insufficient visual understanding and a lack of conceptual knowledge [8].
