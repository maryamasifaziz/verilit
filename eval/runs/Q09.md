# What are the main approaches to explaining the predictions of black-box machine learning models?

## Literature review

Explaining the predictions of black-box machine learning models involves a variety of post-hoc techniques, intrinsic approaches, evaluation frameworks, and security considerations. Regarding primary post-hoc interpretability techniques, methods for deep neural networks are often categorized into prototype-based methods, concept-based methods, and other approaches [1]. Additionally, an extended framework of Bayesian networks can generate post-hoc interpretations of black-box predictive models by extracting a Bayesian network as an approximation for a specific prediction [2]. 

When comparing intrinsic white-box modeling approaches to post-hoc explanations, a meta-analysis of 67 studies in medical imaging demonstrates a consistent 5 to 7 percent AUC performance penalty for interpretable models [3]. Furthermore, evaluation on the PatchCamelyon dataset showed that SmoothGrad and Occlusion have a statistically bigger overlap with the intrinsically interpretable ProtoPNet, whereas Deconvolution and LIME have the least overlap [4]. 

To assess explanation quality, standard benchmarks and evaluation metrics are utilized across different domains. One study evaluates the explanation quality of video DeepFake classifiers using the DFDC and DFD datasets [5], while the Consistency-Aware Explainable AI framework tests explanation consistency using the UCI Heart Disease dataset with Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost classifiers [6]. 

Despite these advancements, current explanation methods face significant limitations and vulnerabilities. Post-hoc explanations based on perturbations, such as LIME, exhibit large instability, posing challenges to their effectiveness and harming user trust [7]. Specifically, LIME relies on local perturbation strategies that are prone to data structure distortion in high-dimensional sparse data, resulting in distribution shifts that reduce explanation stability [8].
