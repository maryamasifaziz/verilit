# How does instruction tuning affect the zero-shot abilities of language models?

## Literature review

Instruction tuning is a prominent technique used to enhance the capabilities of language models. Primary training methods include utilizing specific datasets such as DoIT for Chinese large language models, which covers over 40,000 instruction instances across ten abilities [1]. Additionally, the Self-refine Instruction-tuning method employs a two-stage process that first transfers reasoning abilities through synthetic demonstration instruction-tuning, followed by preference optimization using Direct Preference Optimization [2]. In the multimodal domain, Vision-Flan serves as a visual instruction tuning dataset comprising 187 diverse tasks and 1,664,261 instances from academic datasets [6], while VisIT-Bench is utilized to evaluate instruction-following vision-language models with 592 test queries and 70 instruction families [5].

Instruction tuning significantly impacts zero-shot capabilities. It enhances zero-shot generalization across various tasks while improving specific task performance [3]. Furthermore, cross-lingual instruction tuning consistently improves zero-shot generalization, outperforming baselines by average scores of 20.7% for English and 13.6% for Korean [4].

Despite these benefits, instruction tuning has notable limitations and negative side effects. Full-parameter instruction tuning can cause knowledge degradation and increase hallucinations by inaccurately borrowing tokens from conceptually similar instances [7]. Additionally, traditional instruction tuning inadvertently conditions models to fabricate answers instead of communicating their epistemic limitations when faced with queries outside their knowledge boundaries [8].
