# How well do graph neural networks perform on molecular property prediction?

## Literature review

Graph neural networks (GNNs) utilize a variety of architectural methods and techniques for molecular property prediction. These include developing a neural architecture search approach to automate the design of message-passing neural networks (MPNNs) [1] and employing Gated Graph Recursive Neural Networks, which model molecules as directed complete graphs with spatial atom positions using a recursive neural network equipped with a simple gating function and skip connections [2].

In terms of predictive performance and benchmarks, specific GNN configurations demonstrate competitive or superior results in chemistry. Attention and Edge Memory schemes benchmarked across eight physical-chemical and bioactivity datasets perform on par with state-of-the-art machine learning methods while setting a new standard on sparse multi-task virtual screening targets [3]. Additionally, Multi-View Graph Neural Network (MV-GNN) models achieve superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art models across a variety of challenging molecular property prediction benchmarks [4].

Despite these successes, applying graph neural networks to molecular data faces notable limitations, bottlenecks, and challenges. Two primary challenges in compound property and activity prediction are compound representation and model interpretability [5]. Furthermore, the predictive performance of graph neural networks for chemical properties is restricted by the "curse of depth" [6].
