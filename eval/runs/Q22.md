# What are membership inference attacks against machine learning models and what defenses exist?

## Literature review

Membership inference attacks against machine learning models can be studied through systematic frameworks like Membership-Doctor and ML-Doctor, which establish threat model taxonomies [1, 2]. Notably, these attacks do not require strict threat model assumptions such as same-architecture or same-distribution between shadow and target models [1]. Regarding the specific vulnerabilities of models, training data samples that are susceptible to membership inference in non-overfitted models often present as class outliers, such as hard-to-classify or noisy data [3]. Furthermore, research indicates that the training data of fully trained machine learning models generally exhibits lower prediction sensitivities than non-training data [4].

To protect models against membership inference, various defenses and mitigation strategies have been developed. Differential privacy serves as a defense mechanism, though it frequently leads to high accuracy losses [3]. Additionally, techniques like AdaMixup use adaptive mixup during training to improve model robustness and decrease membership inference risks [5].

Mitigating membership inference involves carefully balancing privacy and model utility. Under strict privacy constraints, perturbing input training data results in a better utility tradeoff compared to adding noise later in the machine learning pipeline [6]. Moreover, employing Bounded ReLU activation functions in differentially private models trained via DPSGD helps preserve utility, enabling them to achieve accuracies such as 96.02% on MNIST, 84.76% on FashionMnist, and 44.42% on CIFAR-10 [7].
