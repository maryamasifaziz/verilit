# What techniques such as quantization reduce the inference cost of large language models?

## Literature review

Various techniques are employed to reduce the inference cost, memory footprint, and computational overhead of large language models. Regarding quantization, GPTQ is utilized to compress models into 4-bit representation for enhanced storage efficiency and faster inference [1]. Additionally, 4-bit quantization techniques such as nf4 and fp4 demonstrate comparable attributes concerning inference speed, memory consumption, and content quality [2]. When combining post-training 4-bit weight quantization with knowledge distillation, the memory footprint and inference latency are reduced even further [6].

Pruning and sparsity methods are also critical for model optimization. Sparsity Induction drives models toward increased sparsity prior to pruning by leveraging distribution-level mathematically equivalent scaling transformations and feature-level Spectral Norm Loss [3]. Furthermore, ETAPS executes gradual pruning of large language models by enforcing a tightening global sparsity constraint across progressive stages while simultaneously training a sparse delta constrained to a small, fixed budget [4].

Finally, compression and distillation methods address inference overhead through structured knowledge transfer. The feature alignment-based knowledge distillation algorithm uses both a multi-layer feature alignment strategy and a multi-task loss function to transfer knowledge from large pre-trained models to lightweight student models, which significantly cuts down computational overhead and storage requirements [5].
