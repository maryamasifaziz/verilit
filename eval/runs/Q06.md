# What techniques make deep neural networks robust to adversarial examples?

## Literature review

Improving the adversarial robustness of deep neural networks involves several primary algorithmic and training methods. One such approach is a two-stage training procedure that maps input samples into a feature embedding space with high separability initially, and then learns parameters exclusively for the final classification layer [1]. Additionally, the Adversarial Noise Propagation (ANP) training algorithm enhances robustness by injecting noise into hidden layers in a layer-wise manner [2]. 

Standard datasets and benchmarking suites are essential for evaluating these capabilities. RobustBench serves as a standardized benchmark evaluating image classification models across $l_\infty$- and $l_2$-threat models as well as common corruptions [4]. Furthermore, ARES-Bench provides a comprehensive robustness benchmark for image classification on ImageNet utilizing 55 typical deep learning models [3]. 

Regarding performance trade-offs and empirical results, random sampling in the DARTS search space combined with simple ensembling improves robustness to PGD attacks by nearly 12% [5]. In the context of quantization, post-training quantization suffers from substantial accuracy loss in noisy environments, whereas quantization-aware training successfully preserves model accuracy while keeping inference latency low [6]. 

Despite these advancements, existing defense mechanisms face notable limitations and vulnerabilities. Reliable evaluation of defenses is frequently constrained to manual expert crafting or fixed attack ensembles that might lack effectiveness against a given defense [7]. Moreover, defense methods in federated learning are easily circumvented by strong adaptive adversaries that simultaneously target multiple objectives, and these defenses demonstrate limited effectiveness when operating without assumptions regarding underlying data distributions [8].
