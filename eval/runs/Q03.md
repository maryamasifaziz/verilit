# How effective is chain-of-thought prompting for multi-step reasoning in language models?

*VeriLit (with verification) · gemini gemini-3.5-flash-lite · 8 claims, 8 verified, 0 unverified · 215 s*

## Literature review

Chain-of-thought prompting is a technique that encourages a large language model to think step by step to improve performance on reasoning tasks [1]. Additionally, synthetic prompting leverages a few handcrafted examples to prompt the model to generate more chain-of-thought demonstrations by itself using alternating backward and forward processes [2]. 

Regarding performance across multi-step benchmarks, prompting a 540B-parameter language model with eight chain-of-thought exemplars achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on the GSM8K benchmark of math word problems [3]. Furthermore, self-consistency decoding boosts the performance of chain-of-thought prompting on arithmetic and commonsense reasoning benchmarks, including GSM8K (+17.9%), SVAMP (+11.0%), AQuA (+12.2%), StrategyQA (+6.4%), and ARC-challenge (+3.9%) [4]. 

In terms of primary reported results and gains compared to standard prompting, chain-of-thought prompting generally improves average performance by a small amount for non-reasoning models, while for models with explicit reasoning capabilities, it often results in only marginal, if any, gains in answer accuracy [5]. On MMLU, directly generating the answer without chain-of-thought leads to almost identical accuracy compared to using chain-of-thought unless the question or model's response contains an equals sign [6]. 

However, several limitations and failure modes are associated with this approach. Chain-of-thought reasoning writes steps as text and follows one reasoning path at a time, which limits model capabilities [7]. Moreover, in Multimodal Large Language Models, chain-of-thought prompting exhibits failure modes including premature answer commitment and limited direct visual-token access during rationale generation [8].
