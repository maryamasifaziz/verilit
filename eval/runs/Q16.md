# How is reinforcement learning from human feedback used to align language models and what are its limitations?

## Literature review

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) is used to align language models through various core techniques and methodologies. For instance, Off-Policy Corrected Reward Modeling (OCRM) employs importance weighting to iteratively off-policy correct the reward model to handle overoptimization [1], while RRHF uses the logarithm of conditional probabilities to score sampled responses, aligning them with human preferences via ranking loss [2]. To support this process, large-scale preference datasets and benchmarks are employed. Skywork-Reward-V2 features the SynPref-40M dataset comprising 40 million preference pairs alongside a suite of eight reward models trained on a subset of 26 million pairs [4], and Multimodal RewardBench provides 5,211 expert-annotated triplets spanning six domains including general correctness, preference, knowledge, reasoning, safety, and visual question-answering [3].

Empirical results demonstrate notable improvements in model behavior through enhanced alignment techniques. Reward models trained on filtered data utilizing the Preference Difference (PD) metric attain higher calibrated accuracy and improved RLHF performance across three open-source models [5]. Furthermore, the Value-based Calibration (VCB) method outperforms existing alignment approaches on summarization and AI assistant datasets, demonstrating strong generalizability, robustness, and diversity [6].

Despite these advancements, RLHF faces several current limitations, failure modes, and safety challenges. Studies note that RLHF suffers from model misspecification, incorrect generalization, and sparse feedback [7]. Additionally, when human feedback relies solely on partial observations, it can lead to specific failure cases such as overjustification and deceptive inflation [8].
